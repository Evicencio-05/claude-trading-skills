### 2a. Company Overview

**Business Model:** Micron designs and manufactures semiconductor memory — DRAM (Dynamic Random Access Memory), NAND Flash, and NOR Flash — plus High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Unlike fabless peers, Micron controls its own fabs in Idaho, Virginia, Japan, Singapore, and Taiwan, plus new CHIPS Act-funded fabs under construction in Idaho and New York.

| Automotive & Embedded | $2.7B | Moderate |

**Competitive Moat:**
- Only U.S. manufacturer of DRAM and NAND at scale — geopolitical moat
- ~23% global DRAM market share (vs. SK Hynix ~35%, Samsung ~40%)
- HBM production capacity is constrained by advanced packaging — Micron leveraging unique architecture for HBM3E/HBM4
- CHIPS Act grants ($6.4B) + loan guarantees create a subsidized domestic manufacturing advantage
- R&D scale: ~$3B/year, 1-gamma node DRAM in volume production

**TAM:** Global DRAM TAM ~$120B in 2026 (up from ~$75B in 2025); HBM segment alone ~$62B in 2026 (per TrendForce estimates)

**Key Growth Drivers:**
1. **HBM/AI Infrastructure:** Every NVIDIA Blackwell system requires 192GB+ of HBM3E; AI capex cycles are driving insatiable demand with no near-term supply relief
2. **Memory Upcycle Pricing:** DRAM spot prices projected up 40-50% in H1 2026; structural shortage conditions, not just a cyclical bump
3. **CHIPS Act Domestic Manufacturing:** New Idaho and New York fabs backed by ~$6.4B in grants — capacity expansion ahead of Chinese competition restrictions

**Key Risks:**
1. **Memory Cycle Reversal (2027):** Samsung P4L and SK Hynix M15X fabs expected in volume production by 2027 — risk of oversupply
2. **China Export Restrictions:** China's CAC previously restricted critical infrastructure operators from buying Micron products; ongoing export control dynamics
3. **Capex Intensity:** $25B+ planned capex in FY2026 — generates enormous cash, but if cycle turns, stranded assets are a concern
