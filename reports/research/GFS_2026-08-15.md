## 2. Business & Products

**2a. Business model**
Contract semiconductor manufacturing — wafer starts, masks, and design enablement on specialty/mature nodes. Customers buy GF when they need RF-SOI, FD-SOI, BCD/power, SiGe, or silicon photonics with geographic diversification (U.S., Europe, Singapore) rather than bleeding-edge logic.

**2b. Products & technology**
Platforms: RF-SOI (mobile FEM), silicon photonics / SiGe (AI optical networking), BCD/GaN/IVR (power delivery), RISC-V IP stack (post–Synopsys ARC + MIPS). Q2 R&D $174M (~9.7% of revenue), up from $134M YoY. Roadmap: SCALE optical / CPO-LPO, capacity adds for oversubscribed SiGe through 2027, Quantum Technology Solutions with expected DoC support. Mid-cycle specialty platform; photonics still early relative to total mix.

**2c. TAM & growth runway**
Global foundry market remains TSMC-dominated; GF’s served specialty TAM is a multi-year runway if optical networking and power for AI servers keep compounding. Management raised 2026 data-center/comms growth to 50–60% YoY — but that end market is still ~16% of revenue, so company-level growth stays mid-single digits until mix scales.

GF wins when customers need U.S./EU capacity, RF-SOI, or photonics/SiGe differentiation. It loses on pure cost mature CMOS vs UMC/SMIC and cannot contest sub-7nm logic vs TSMC. Gap vs TSMC is structural; gap vs UMC is contestable on specialty IP.

**3c. Moat**
Intangible assets (process IP, PDKs, single-source designs) + efficient scale in RF-SOI/photonics + geographic/policy positioning (CHIPS). Moat stable-to-strengthening in optical networking; eroding if mature-node pricing war intensifies.

**3d. Primary competitive threat**
TSMC (and increasingly Intel Foundry) capturing AI-adjacent optical/power sockets with broader ecosystems, or UMC undercutting on overlapping mature nodes while GF’s largest end market (smart mobile) stays soft.
