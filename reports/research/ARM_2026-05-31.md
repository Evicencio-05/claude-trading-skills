## 2. Business & Products

### 2a. Business Model

**License + royalty IP model:** Customers pay upfront licensing fees for CPU/GPU/system IP designs, then per-chip royalties on every unit shipped. **97%+ gross margin.** Two revenue streams: licensing (lumpy, contract-driven) and royalties (recurring, volume-driven).

### 2b. Products & Technology

- **Armv9 architecture:** ~2x royalty rate vs Armv8; deployed in Apple M-series, Qualcomm Snapdragon, Nvidia Vera
- **Compute Subsystems (CSS):** Higher take-rate bundled IP packages
- **Arm AGI CPU:** First in-house production silicon for agentic AI data centers; Meta lead partner; OpenAI, Cloudflare, SAP, SK Telecom committed; **>$2B demand FY27–28**
- **Arm Metis:** Open-source agentic AI security framework (May 2026)
- **Neoverse V3:** Data center CPU IP already shipping

R&D: 8,058 engineers of 9,584 total employees (+16% YoY).

### 2c. TAM

CPU TAM expanding via AI — Nvidia sees **$200B CPU market opportunity**. Arm targets **$15B revenue by FY2031** (from $4.92B today). Hyperscaler custom silicon (AWS Graviton, Google Axion, Microsoft Cobalt) accelerating Arm share toward **~50% of top hyperscaler CPU compute**.

## 3. Competitive Intelligence

| Company | Ticker | Role | vs ARM |
|---------|--------|------|--------|
| **Arm Holdings** | ARM | CPU IP leader | Baseline |
| RISC-V International | — | Open ISA | Royalty-free; ~25% aggregate share 2026; rising in auto/IoT |
| Intel | INTC | x86 + foundry | Losing server share; IFS catch-up |
| SNPS / Cadence | SNPS/CDNS | EDA/IP | Complementary EDA vendors; ARM +177% YTD vs peers |
| Qualcomm | QCOM | Licensee + competitor | Legal dispute; Ventana acquisition for RISC-V path |

**Moat:** Switching costs (2–3 year design cycles), ecosystem (180B chips), Armv9 royalty step-up. **Strengthening** in data center; **eroding at margin** in low-end MCUs from RISC-V.

**Primary threat:** **RISC-V** — royalty-free, geopolitically favored (China, EU sovereignty); Qualcomm's Ventana acquisition signals licensee defection risk. Near-term: **FTC antitrust probe** (May 15) examining whether Arm favors own silicon over licensees.
