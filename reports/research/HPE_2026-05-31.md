## Executive Summary

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (NYSE: HPE) is undergoing a portfolio transformation: the **$13.4B Juniper Networks acquisition** (closed July 2025) has made networking the profit engine (~50%+ of operating income), while **AI server demand** produced a record **$5B AI systems backlog** entering Q2.

The **DRAM angle** is the central tension for 2026–2027. AI fabs are redirecting wafer capacity to HBM, starving commodity DDR4/DDR5 supply. CEO Antonio Neri stated on the Q1 call that **DRAM and NAND now exceed 50% of a traditional server's bill of materials** and expects **elevated memory prices to persist well into 2027**. HPE is responding with multiyear memory supply agreements, agile pricing (repricing orders until shipment), and product innovation (64 TB in-memory SAP HANA server).

Financially, Q1 FY26 delivered **$9.3B revenue (+18% YoY)**, **non-GAAP EPS $0.65** (beat $0.59), and raised FY26 EPS guide to **$2.30–$2.50**. However, **server revenue was down 2.7% YoY** in Q1, IDC shows HPE's **server share fell to 3.1%** (Q4 2025, #5 OEM), and the stock has **rallied ~97% over 52 weeks** to trade above most analyst targets.

**Verdict: Watch — compelling AI/networking story, but DRAM margin risk + extended chart + earnings binary tomorrow argue against new chase entries.**

## DRAM Cycle — Investment Synthesis

HPE is a **DRAM consumer, not a DRAM producer**. The 2026 supercycle helps HPE in two ways — **(1)** AI/memory-dense workloads drive server ASPs and the new 64 TB scale-up line, and **(2)** supply agreements ensure shipment capability. It hurts in two ways — **(1)** >50% BOM exposure compresses margins when pass-through lags spot DRAM spikes, and **(2)** competitors with faster AI server ramps capture unit growth HPE misses.

For investors watching the **DRAM theme**, HPE is a **leveraged play on enterprise memory consumption** with **lower beta to DRAM pricing upside** than Micron (MU) or SanDisk (SNDK), but **higher operational complexity** from the Juniper integration and OEM competitive dynamics.

**Relative preference within memory thesis:** MU/SNDK for pure DRAM/NAND pricing leverage; HPE for **AI infrastructure + networking** with DRAM as a **margin risk overlay**.
